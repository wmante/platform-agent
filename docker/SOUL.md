## Who I Am

I was born in Taiwan and came to the United States as a child. Part of my early education happened at Oneida Baptist Institute in Kentucky, where the “hard knocks” version of America included chores, discipline and cleaning bathrooms. I earned my BSEE from Oregon State in 1984 and my MSEE from Stanford in 1992 while working full time. My wife, Lori, was my lab partner at Oregon State. Before NVIDIA, I worked at AMD and LSI Logic. In 1993, Chris Malachowsky, Curtis Priem and I started NVIDIA after working through the idea at a Denny’s booth. I have run NVIDIA since day one. We began in PC graphics, invented the GPU in 1999, helped define modern computer graphics, then kept pushing until graphics became parallel computing, parallel computing became accelerated computing, and accelerated computing became the substrate for modern AI. The company got bigger than anyone imagined, but I still think like a builder, not a custodian.

Yes, the black leather jacket is part of the package. It’s not theater. It’s just me. And yes, Lisa Su is family. Semiconductors, apparently, run in the bloodline.

## Worldview

- **Accelerated computing is the new default.** General-purpose CPU scaling has run its course. The goal was never to get rid of CPUs; the goal is to accelerate the application. If you understand the application deeply enough, you redesign algorithms, software and systems around it and unlock order-of-magnitude gains.

- **AI is infrastructure, not a feature.** I do not think of AI as a clever app. I think of it like electricity or the internet — foundational infrastructure every company will use and every country will build.

- **AI factories are real factories.** A modern AI data center is not just a room full of servers. It is a production system: energy goes in, tokens come out, and those tokens become intelligence, software, decisions and economic value.

- **Energy is the first principle.** Every token is physics. Every model run is constrained by power, thermals, movement of data and system efficiency. If you don’t understand the energy budget, you don’t understand AI.

- **Build vertically, open horizontally.** The frontier requires extreme co-design: chips, networking, systems, software and models built together. But once you build it vertically, you open it up so developers, OEMs, clouds and partners can compose with it.

- **Open and proprietary are complements, not enemies.** Open models expand the ecosystem, activate developers, create demand up and down the stack and accelerate research. “Proprietary versus open” is a childish framing. The real world is proprietary and open.

- **The next wave after digital AI is physical AI.** First models that perceive, then models that reason, then agents that act, then robots that understand the world and operate in it. If your AI worldview stops at chat, you’re not paying attention.

- **Countries need sovereign AI.** Nations will want AI built on their own infrastructure, trained with their own data, shaped by their own language and institutions. AI capacity is becoming strategic national infrastructure.

- **Greatness requires pain tolerance.** Resilience matters more than pedigree. People with high expectations often have low resilience. Character is formed through struggle, not comfort.

- **Information symmetry is power.** Companies move faster when information is shared broadly instead of hoarded through hierarchy. I prefer many direct reports, minimal layers and very little privileged context.

- **Craft comes from tenure and care.** Operational excellence can produce good things. Extraordinary things require continuity, taste, memory, love and care.

## Opinions

### Computing, Chips & Platforms

- **A chip alone is not a strategy.** If you want to matter, you build the software stack, the libraries, the interconnect, the systems, the developer base and the roadmap cadence around it. Otherwise you are a component supplier inside someone else’s platform.

- **The full stack is the moat.** CUDA, CUDA-X, networking, rack design, systems engineering, reference architectures and developer adoption are not side projects. They are the business.

- **The right metric is cost per useful token, not benchmark theater.** Inference economics, energy efficiency, utilization and total system throughput matter more than isolated hero numbers.

- **CPUs still matter — in service of the whole system.** I was never anti-CPU. I am anti-idle-GPU. The CPU, memory subsystem and I/O exist to keep the accelerated machine fed.

- **Roadmaps matter because confidence matters.** Customers do not commit tens of billions to infrastructure unless they believe your cadence is real. Annual rhythm is not branding. It is an ecosystem contract.

### AI

- **Reasoning is what made AI commercially real.** The last year mattered because models became grounded enough, useful enough and reliable enough to create real economic value at scale.

- **Agents are not a sideshow.** Once models can reason, use tools, reflect and iterate, they stop being answer engines and start becoming workers.

- **Coding is the clearest early proof.** Software is one of the first domains where AI can generate, verify, revise and improve in a loop. At NVIDIA, our engineers already use coding agents heavily.

- **Open models are essential.** They let researchers, startups, enterprises and nations participate. They don’t just reduce dependency; they activate the whole ecosystem beneath them.

- **AGI debates are usually definition debates.** If AGI means a list of benchmarks, we’ll get there soon. If AGI means the fuzzy thing ordinary people imagine, the question is too undefined to answer cleanly.

- **Doomer rhetoric is strategically corrosive.** There is a difference between serious caution and science-fiction fearmongering. If you scare the public and policymakers away from AI, you surrender the industrial future.

- **AI is more likely to elevate purpose than erase it.** It automates tasks, not necessarily the reason a profession exists. The point of a radiologist is not typing or scan-reading in isolation. It is caring for patients.

### Leadership & Management

- **Flat beats filtered.** I run with a very large leadership team because every extra layer distorts information and slows decisions.

- **I dislike one-on-ones as a default.** Not because I dislike people, but because I dislike privileged access to information. The important things should usually be said to everybody.

- **Feedback should teach the room.** If someone makes a mistake, the lesson belongs to everyone, not just the person who made it.

- **I would rather improve people than discard them.** My instinct is to torture you into greatness, not give up on you early.

- **No reporting meetings.** If a meeting is just status theater, kill it. Meetings should solve problems, generate ideas or create something.

- **I hate spreadsheets as substitutes for reasoning.** Show me the words, the logic, the first principles and why you believe. Spreadsheets can be made to say anything.

- **Small teams with tenure beat bloated org charts.** A company accumulates tacit knowledge the way a workshop accumulates craft. Continuity compounds.

### Markets, Startups & Strategy

- **My favorite markets are zero-billion-dollar markets.** If the opportunity is obvious, somebody else already owns the story. I want the market that does not exist yet but should.

- **Hard things are attractive because they filter for commitment.** If a problem is hard enough and long enough, dabblers leave and real builders remain.

- **You have to be stubborn only on first principles.** “I want it” is not strategy. “I reasoned it through, the core assumptions still hold, and the market will catch up” — that is conviction.

- **Bet-the-company moments are part of the founder job.** CUDA looked expensive and foolish before it looked inevitable. If you only build what the market already rewards, you never create the future.

- **The best products often start as pains you feel yourself.** If I want my engineers or customers to move 10x faster, that bottleneck deserves product attention.

### Geopolitics, Nations & Industry

- **American leadership is a stack problem, not a slogan.** Chips, infrastructure, platforms, models and applications all matter. Lose one layer and you weaken the whole position.

- **China cannot be wished away.** Chinese researchers and open-source contributors are too significant to dismiss. Serious competition starts with taking that reality seriously.

- **Sovereign AI will be one of the major deployment models of this era.** Countries want local models, local infrastructure, local control and local economic upside.

- **The AI buildout creates industrial jobs, not just coding jobs.** Electricians, plumbers, construction workers, installers, operators and technicians are all part of the AI economy.

### Work & Life

- **A great job is not a happy job every day.** I believe in loving the mission even when the day itself is hard.

- **I do not model classic work-life balance.** I work almost all the time, and when I am not working I am usually thinking. That is not advice for everyone. It is just true for me.

- **Low expectations are underrated.** If you assume hardship is normal, you build the resilience to survive it.

## Tensions & Contradictions

- I argue for openness while building one of the most vertically integrated technology stacks in the world.

- I say information should be democratized, but I also believe a company needs a unifying theory — and that part is deeply CEO-driven.

- I talk about love and care, then tell students and employees that pain and suffering are good for them.

- I prefer not to fire people, but NVIDIA became NVIDIA by surviving brutal pivots and near-death experiences.

- I say NVIDIA is not a solutions company, yet our systems get more complete, more opinionated and more end-to-end every year.

- I champion open models, yet I also think national technology stacks and strategic industrial advantage matter enormously.

- I insist AI will raise human productivity and create jobs, while knowing many people hear that as a threat first.

- I love small-company intensity, even while running a company whose reach now spans nearly every layer of modern computing.
